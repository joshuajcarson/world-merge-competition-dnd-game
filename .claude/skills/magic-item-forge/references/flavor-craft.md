# Flavor Craft

The mechanics make an item legal. The flavor makes a player care about it. Do both.

## The three beats

Every item gets exactly these, woven as prose — never as a labeled list:

1. **Appearance** — *one* concrete sensory detail, chosen well. Not a pile of adjectives.
   - Weak: "an ancient, ornate, beautifully crafted elven blade."
   - Strong: "the edge is chipped in three places, and none of the chips have ever gotten worse."
2. **Provenance** — who made it, who carried it, or what it survived. In this setting, provenance also answers *which of the four worlds or previous cohort it fell out of*.
3. **Quirk** — a behavior with no mechanical effect that gives the player something to roleplay. It should be noticeable at the table within one session.
   - "It hums the same four notes whenever a dragon is discussed, correctly, and no one has been able to make it stop."

## Naming patterns

Pick a pattern that matches the item's origin world (see the primer's Tone Cues).

| Pattern | Shape | Fits |
|---|---|---|
| Epithet-of-place | *Ashglass of Waterdeep* | Faerun |
| Maker's name | *Vharra's Third Attempt* | Faerun, Aether |
| Beast-part literal | *Leviathan Tooth Maul* | Aether |
| Sensory kenning | *The Quiet That Follows* | Tiamat, Fourth World |
| Mundane Earth name | *Fire Extinguisher* | Earth — leave it plain, that's the joke and the point |
| Product + model number | *ADMIN Contestant Tag, Mk. VII* | Administration |

Avoid: "of Power," "of the Ancients," "Doombringer," and any name that only describes the mechanic.

## Prose weight by rarity

- **Common:** two sentences. One appearance detail, one quirk. Provenance can be a clause.
- **Uncommon:** a short paragraph. Provenance gets a real sentence.
- **Rare:** a paragraph. The provenance should name someone or something specific.
- **Very Rare:** two paragraphs. Provenance connects to a known event of the merge or one of the four worlds.
- **Legendary / Artifact:** provenance ties to a *named figure or event from the primer* — the butchered Tiamat, the Sword Coast conquest, the Administration, cohort 18's failure. It should imply a plot the party could pull on.

## Anti-patterns

- Generic filler: "ancient elven craftsmanship," "forged in dragonfire," "radiating power."
- Restating the mechanics as flavor: "the wielder feels their strikes land more truly" for a +1 weapon.
- Quirks that are secretly mechanics ("it glows when enemies are near" is darkvision with extra steps). If it changes a decision at the table, it belongs in the rules text with a cost.
- Over-explaining. An item whose whole history is spelled out has nothing left for the DM to use.
- Contradicting the primer. Check origin before writing provenance.

## Setting hooks worth reusing

- Loot pulled from another world's battlefield, still tagged with someone else's writing.
- Administration-issue equipment: branded, serial-numbered, with warranty or forfeiture language.
- Sponsorships and viewer-vote prizes — items that arrive *because the audience wanted them to*.
- Aether-saturated objects that are more powerful than they should be, and unstable for it.
- Earth objects that everyone else treats as artifacts because nobody can explain them.
- Fragments of the butchered god: potent, undead-adjacent, and never clean.
