# Mall Security Keyring

*Wondrous Item, Uncommon*

This keyring has 2 charges and regains 1d2 expended charges daily at dawn.

**Secure the Premises.** As a Magic action, you can touch a door, window, gate, chest, or other object that can be closed and locked and expend 1 charge. The object swings shut and locks. While it is locked this way, opening it requires a successful DC 15 Strength (Athletics) check to force, or a DC 15 Dexterity (Sleight of Hand) check with thieves' tools to pick. A *knock* spell or comparable magic suppresses the lock for 10 minutes rather than ending it. The lock lasts for 8 hours.

While holding the keyring, you can end a lock you created by touching the object (no action required), and you can open any object you have locked with the keyring as if you held its key.

The keyring has no effect on creatures, and it cannot seal an opening that a creature is currently standing in.

## Flavor

Seven keys and a scuffed plastic fob on a steel ring, the fob's lettering worn down to *GU ST SERV CES* by a thumb that rubbed it raw during long shifts. It belonged to Steven McDouglas, a night shift guard at Westfield Mall — a man whose uniform looked enough like a real one to get him laughed at, who wrote up violations no one ever collected, and who, on his last shift before the worlds merged, decided that if no one would enforce the rules he would; he locked a teenager in a janitor's closet for skateboarding in the promenade. The ring will not sit still. Left on a table it shifts and jangles softly whenever nobody is looking at it directly, and it goes dead silent for a moment any time someone addresses its bearer by a title of rank.

## DM Notes

- **Why it's here:** salvage from the Earth-side merge line — a shopping-mall fragment that came through mostly intact. Good early loot from a scavenged building, or sold cheap by someone who found the closet and did not like what was in it.
- **Hooks:** the closet arrived too. The door is still locked, the keyring still opens it, and whether the teen is dead, alive, or something the merge made of them is the DM's call. The guard himself is unaccounted for — an NPC who now has, for the first time, actual power to make people obey him.
- **Balance note:** an 8-hour lock at DC 15 can shut down a chase or a dungeon route. That's the point at Tier 1, but it cuts both ways — NPCs and monsters can take the ring, and two charges a day means the party must choose which door matters.
