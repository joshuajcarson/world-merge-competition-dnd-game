# Grinder

## Description

Was Marcus Webb, a rideshare driver working the overnight shift when the merge
took him. Nobody uses that name anymore, least of all him. The
[Ashvein Company](../factions/ashvein-company.md)'s duergar call him Grinder and
he answers to it, chained through a warded collar that reads more like a leash
than a lock. Before every serious fight, checks his bare wrist for a watch that
isn't there — a tic left over from a life spent tracking fares between
pickups, the one piece of Marcus Webb still running underneath everything the
duergar have made of him.

## What the Party Knows

Nothing yet. See DM Only for how and when this changes.

## DM Only

**How the duergar use him.** The [Ashvein Company](../factions/ashvein-company.md)
worked out early that [Overcharge](../races/fulgurites.md) triggers on being
bloodied — so before a serious raid, they wound him themselves, on their own
terms, and send him in already charged. He opens fights they want opened hard.
Whether he has anything left to give the party when they meet him depends
entirely on whether the duergar already spent it before this scene starts —
DM's call, and worth deciding deliberately rather than defaulting to "still
loaded."

**Personality under it.** Not broken, not compliant — furious, and worn down
past the point where the fury has anywhere to go. He doesn't talk to the
duergar beyond what keeps the collar from hurting. If the party ever gets him
alone and gives him an actual opening, that changes; nobody has yet.

**The Agent hook — read this before running any Briarwood-side scene with
him.** If the table's engagement with the Briarwood arc dips — a session that
runs quiet, a stretch without a strong escalation — [Agent](agent.md) is built
to notice and do something about it (see that file: it "constantly looks to
create drama" and personally controls fragment/timing placement). Teleporting
the entire [Ashvein Company](../factions/ashvein-company.md), Grinder included,
from the Underdark to the parking lot or service corridor of
[Briarwood Mall](../places/briarwood-mall.md) is a ready-made, high-impact way
to do that: an unscheduled, unexplained arrival of armed duergar and a
Fulgurite berserker, played for shock, with Agent narrating it live as a
ratings save. This should land as a genuine surprise the first time it's used —
nothing in Briarwood's established geography telegraphs the Underdark at all,
which is exactly why it works as an Agent move rather than an organic
escalation. Hold this in reserve rather than scheduling it; it's a lever for a
DM to pull, not a plotted story beat.

**On arrival at Briarwood.** The duergar are badly out of their depth on the
surface — no tunnels, no home terrain, no idea what a mall is — and should
play disoriented and aggressive rather than tactically sharp. Grinder is not:
he's spent his captivity as a weapon aimed at whatever the duergar pointed him
at, and being pointed at something new doesn't cost him anything. If this scene
happens, it's a strong moment for the party to consider freeing him rather than
just fighting him — a captive shock-trooper cut loose from his handlers, mid-fight,
is a legitimate wildcard, not guaranteed to side with anyone.

**Statblock notes.** No tier fixed — build him as a berserker-leaning melee
reskin, heavier and more reckless than [Priya Anand](priya-anand.md)'s
controller build, with the standard [Overcharge](../races/fulgurites.md) trait
and nothing else layered on. His danger is straightforward: he hits hard, he
doesn't retreat, and the duergar have already decided his life is worth less
than the raid succeeding.
