# Miriel Ashgrove

## Description

A half-elf in her late twenties, ink to the second knuckle on both hands, working
the registry table one over from [Halric Ammakyl](halric-ammakyl.md)'s muster
tables at [Scornubel](../places/scornubel.md)'s Council Yard. She checks every
mercenary company's contract token against her ledger *twice* before waving anyone
through to the ration stores — once against the page, once against her own memory
— because she caught someone altering a page in it a tenday ago and has trusted
the ledger alone exactly zero times since. She hasn't slept indoors in over a
week. She still says thank you to every refugee who reaches her table, by name,
once she's learned it, and she learns it fast.

## What the Party Knows

Met in person in [Session 3](../../sessions/03-the-flooded-crown.md), at
[the Color Game](../../encounters/the-color-game.md) — the Coster Council
pressed her into standing as Scornubel's "Queen" on an hour's notice, and
she made no secret of resenting it. The party's read on her so far is
positive: overworked, unhappy about the crown, and genuine in a way that's
already earned some goodwill.

## DM Only

**What she actually wants.** Scornubel to survive its own hired protection. She
believes, probably naively, that the abuses can be documented and fixed inside the
existing system — Council contracts, conduct clauses, her own private ledger of
names, companies, dates, and what was taken — rather than solved by anyone burning
the system down. That belief is the whole character; it's what makes her worth
allying with and it's exactly what a party looking to destabilize Scornubel would
need to break or route around.

**Her leverage.** The private ledger is real and it is the single most dangerous
document in Scornubel that nobody with power has bothered to take seriously yet.
It names specific free companies and specific abuses with dates attached. A party
that wants Scornubel's mercenary contingent to fracture from the inside — rather
than fighting a mustered army head-on — could do worse than steal, publish, or
weaponize that ledger. She would rather it be used to force reform than to burn
the city down, and will resist handing it to anyone she reads as wanting the
latter.

**Why a half-elf, specifically.** She isn't fully trusted by the human nobles and
merchant costers she works for — [Halric Ammakyl](halric-ammakyl.md) treats her
as competent help, not a peer — but she's one of a handful of people at the yard
who can talk to elven and half-elven refugees in their own tongue without a
translator slowing the line down, which is precisely the kind of quietly
indispensable that keeps her employed despite being nobody's idea of an insider.

**Statblock notes.** No PC levels; she's dangerous to the *situation*, not to
anyone in melee with her. Reskin the 5.5e Commoner or Noble stat block — AC 10,
HP 4–6, no weapon she has ever drawn in anger — with proficiency in Insight and
Persuasion, and functional Elvish. If the party threatens or attacks her, the
fight should be over in one round; she surrenders, talks, or runs before she
fights. She is never meant to be a combat encounter. She is meant to be a decision.

**What she'd do under pressure.** Negotiate first, always — she's spent a week
negotiating with armed men on behalf of people who have nothing, and she's better
at it than her rank suggests. Threatened directly, she'll try to talk her way out
before she runs, and will hand over the public rolls before she ever hands over
her private ledger. She does not consider herself brave. She has simply run out of
the kind of tired where backing down feels like an option.

**If the party allies with her.** She's a natural post-merge contact: someone who
already keeps meticulous records on which mercenary companies can be trusted and
which can't, and who has both the standing and the motive to vouch for the party
to whatever's left of Scornubel's civil authority. She's also the first person on
the Faerun side to have noticed [the architectural anomalies](../lore/deputy-registrars-log-scornubel.md)
that foreshadow the merge itself — which makes her a source of information the
party can't easily get anywhere else on that side of the line.

**If the party kills or removes her.** Nobody else at the Council Yard is
enforcing the conduct clauses, checking contract tokens twice, or keeping an
independent count of refugee abuses. Registration and rationing default to
whichever free company is loudest at the yard that week; the tent city's ordinary
grievances stop having anywhere to go. This is the intended lever for a
destabilization playthrough — she isn't a boss fight, she's a linchpin, and
removing a linchpin quietly is supposed to be more effective than a fight would
be.

**"Queen of Scornubel," confirmed in play (Session 3).** Agent's
royalty-and-regicide decree at the top of
[the Color Game](../../encounters/the-color-game.md) forced the Coster
Council to name someone fast, and Miriel was who they could spare. She
never wanted this and says so. The party's first direct contact with her
happened here rather than through her log — they like her already, which is
a strong asset if the table wants a genuine Scornubel ally rather than a
purely transactional one. Nothing above about her leverage, her ledger, or
what she'd do under pressure changes; the crown is a new complication
layered on top of the same person, not a rewrite of her.
