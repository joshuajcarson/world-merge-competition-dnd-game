# The Chorus

## Description

Whatever number of people were standing close enough together when faerzress
hit them, fused at the moment of the strike instead of surviving it as
separate Fulgurites — too many limbs, moving out of sync with each other, and
a cluster of mismatched faces across its mass that all still try to talk.
Mid-fight, its component voices argue about something utterly mundane — whose
turn it was to pick the kids up, whether a restaurant back home was actually
any good — as if none of them have caught up to what they are now. It is not
one mind pretending to be several. It is several minds, badly overlapped, none
of them fully in control.

## DM Only

**Why it's a solo boss.** The Chorus is what happens when [Overcharge](../races/fulgurites.md)
doesn't happen to one nervous system — it happens to several, fused into one
body with nowhere for the extra charge to go. Play it structurally the way
[the Cindercrest](../../encounters/aether-breach-at-briarwood.md) was played:
a single, high-CR-for-tier threat meant to be survived and outmaneuvered, not
worn down in a straight stand-up fight.

**Compound Overcharge.** Instead of triggering once, the Chorus overcharges
every time it crosses a quarter of its max HP (75%, 50%, 25%) — each fused
person contributing one more discharge before the mass finally goes down. Roll
or choose a different effect (force surge, psychic scream, arc-light) each
time; don't repeat the same one twice in a row if it can be helped, since part
of what should scare a table here is not knowing which one is coming. This
makes the fight naturally phase itself without needing scripted phase
transitions — the HP thresholds do that work.

**Playing it at the table.** Lead with the voices before the violence, the same
way the Aether Breach led with sound before sight — let the party hear it
arguing with itself in the dark before they see what it actually is. Once
combat starts, the arguing doesn't stop; have it talk over its own attacks,
mid-swing, about something nobody in the room can do anything about anymore.
The horror is that it's still, on some level, several ordinary people who
never agreed to become a weapon, and there is no clean way for the party to
save any one of them without ending all of them.

**No mercy option, and that's deliberate.** Unlike a lone Fulgurite or even
[Grinder](grinder.md), there's no version of this fight that ends in the Chorus
walking away rehabilitated — the fusion isn't reversible with anything this
campaign has established, and inventing a cure on the spot would cheapen it.
The honest options are stopping it, or not. If the table wants a mercy beat,
put it in how the fight is narrated (a request the Chorus's voices make near
the end, a plea one of them manages to get out clearly) rather than in a
mechanical out.

**Statblock notes.** No tier fixed — see [Fulgurites](../races/fulgurites.md)'s
open question on challenge band, and size this well above a standard
Fulgurite's CR the same way the Cindercrest ran well above a level 1 party's
weight class. A large-or-bigger multiattacking base with extra HP to support
three full Compound Overcharge triggers is the right shape; the compound
trigger is what makes it a boss; the size and HP just need to survive long
enough for all three to matter.
