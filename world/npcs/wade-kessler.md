# Wade Kessler

## Description

Thirty-four, department-issue haircut, a windbreaker with **DEA** across the back
that he wears specifically so nobody looks twice at him standing near the Radio
Shack stockroom door. He leads with the badge — flips it open before he gives his
name, even in a conversation that has nothing to do with his job, because thirty
seconds of instinctive deference is worth more to him than his own name is.
Tonight he's off the clock, in line for the ThighMaster demo like everyone else,
except he keeps checking his watch against the Orange Julius counter across the
court.

## What the Party Knows

Nothing yet. He's a face in the grand-opening crowd at
[Briarwood Mall](../places/briarwood-mall.md) — nobody has a reason to notice him
over the ThighMaster line.

## DM Only

**The cover.** Kessler works pharmaceutical diversion cases out of the Fort Worth
Resident Office — legitimately the guy who tracks where prescription opioids and
amphetamines go missing between the warehouse and the pharmacy counter. It is the
perfect vantage point to run his own diversion in the other direction: he knows
which shipments are being watched, which routes are clean this week, and how to
make his own product read as "already accounted for" in an evidence log. Four
years in, the case file is the business.

**The operation.** [The Circuit](../factions/the-circuit.md) moves product through
two mall storefronts that look nothing alike on purpose. The
[Radio Shack](../places/briarwood-mall.md) counter is comms and countersurveillance
— a police scanner under the register, and "customers" asking about pager
compatibility are placing orders. The [Orange Julius](../places/briarwood-mall.md)
counter is cash and product, moved in cups; "extra pulp" on an order means the full
weight, no cut. Kessler personally likes that neither business looks like the
other's partner from six feet away.

**Why he's here tonight, personally, instead of running it from a distance.** He
thinks someone in his own crew has been skimming off a shipment for three weeks
running, and the grand-opening crowd is the best cover Briarwood will offer all
year to watch the handoff himself without anyone clocking that he's watching. He
did not plan to still be standing in this building when the world changed.

**Statblock notes.** Human, no levels worth stating pre-merge — he's dangerous
because of the badge and the crew, not his fists. Post-merge, reskin something in
the CR 2–4 band with a command/compulsion-flavored feature: the DEA badge, warped
by the merge the same way [the mall's other objects were](../places/briarwood-mall.md),
now backs up a flashed command with something closer to a *charm* or minor
*compulsion* effect on anyone still wired to defer to authority — which, this
early, is most of the four hundred civilians in the building. Separately, whatever
scanner/radio rig The Circuit was already running out of the Radio Shack stockroom
should plausibly let them intercept chatter across the mall before anyone else can
— see the mall file's note that "every radio in Bellcross" is about to receive the
Administration's broadcast. Kessler's crew gets there first, which is a large part
of how they organize a takeover attempt this fast.

**Act 1 Big Bad.** Once the shock of the merge burns off — after the party has had
at least one session to take the mall's measure — Kessler is built to be the first
antagonist with a face and a plan. He does not want chaos; he wants what he
already had, formalized: he "deputizes" the willing and the scared using the badge
(real authority once, borrowed authority now), taxes what's left of the mall's
supply, and frames it to the other survivors as the only organized security
Briarwood has. He is not wrong that it's organized. He is wrong about who it's
for. This should read as a believable, locally-popular protection racket before it
reads as a villain's plot — that's what makes him worth fighting instead of just a
speed bump.

**The reluctant-ally branch.** Kessler's self-interest, not any change of heart, is
the hinge. If a bigger, external threat shows up that would raze or seize the mall
outright rather than just contest it for control — the [Coster Council](../factions/coster-council.md)'s
hired swords deciding Briarwood is an unguarded warehouse, [Cult of Tiamat](../factions/cult-of-tiamat.md)
cells sweeping through, or the aftermath of something like the
[Aether Breach](../../encounters/aether-breach-at-briarwood.md) escalating — Kessler
has nothing to gain from a burned-down mall. He can pivot to a transactional truce
with the party against that threat without becoming likable: he'll still be running
an angle, still expect the arrangement to end the moment the bigger threat does,
and still be the party's problem again in Act 2 if he survives Act 1. Play the turn
as tactical, not redemptive — he says so himself if asked.

**Under pressure** he negotiates before he fights, stalls before he negotiates, and
always has an exit that isn't through the party. He does not consider himself a
bad guy. That's load-bearing for how he talks.
