# Roy Teasdale

## Description

A soft, unremarkable man in a tie that used to be nice, standing in a grassland valley that
shouldn't have one, directing a herd of rotting dinosaurs with the same flat, aggrieved
competence he probably once denied claims with. Agent calls him **"The Adjustor"** on air. He
answers to it now, which tells you everything about how that went for him.

## What the Party Knows

Nothing yet — first contact is [The Blue Wave](../../encounters/the-blue-wave.md).

## DM Only

**Who he was.** An insurance adjustor from some other Earth fragment, pulled into the merge the
same night as everyone else. He has not let go of November 3rd, 1992 — the night Ross Perot beat
both Bush and Clinton (see [Briarwood Mall](../places/briarwood-mall.md)'s own Session 0: "the
election was Tuesday, Ross Perot won it" — Roy's fragment got the same result, and he has never
once stopped being angry about it). He talks about it unprompted, mid-fight, like it happened
yesterday. For him, emotionally, it did.

**How he got here.** Not established — leave it open, or decide it's as mundane as "aether
exposure did something to him and he leaned into it" the same way everything else on this side of
the merge gets stranger under pressure. He is not a tragic figure the way the Fulgurites are; he
chose this, several times, on the way to becoming what he is now.

**Why Agent likes him.** Agent wanted zombie dinosaurs for the broadcast — the Aether World has
dinosaur megafauna and Agent, per its own established obsessions (see
[Agent](agent.md)'s DM Only, "The Aether world"), loves a stunt that nobody's done in twenty-two
prior cohorts. Roy delivered, using corpses scavenged from an Aether Hornback herd (see
[Aether Hornbacks](../races/aether-hornbacks.md)), and Agent has been feeding him airtime and
attention ever since. He is, in the most literal sense, performing for an audience that thinks his
rage is a bit.

**Playing him.** Genuinely, tediously furious — not a cackling villain, an aggrieved mid-level
bureaucrat who has found a way to make his anger physically dangerous. He monologues about the
election unprompted and does not notice it isn't landing. He is not stupid and he is not
incompetent; he is just a man whose entire personality collapsed into one grudge decades ago and
never found its way back out.

**Statblock.**

### Roy Teasdale, "The Adjustor" (CR 4, 1,100 XP)

*Medium humanoid (human), lawful evil.* **AC** 13 (mage armor-equivalent, natural) · **HP** 71
(13d8+13) · **Speed** 30 ft.
STR 9 (-1) DEX 13 (+1) CON 13 (+1) INT 17 (+3) WIS 12 (+1) CHA 14 (+2).
**Saving Throws** Int +6, Wis +4. **Skills** Arcana +6, Intimidation +5, Persuasion +5.
**Damage Resistance** necrotic. **Senses** darkvision 60 ft., passive Perception 11.
**Languages** Common.

- **Command the Herd.** As a bonus action, Roy orders one Zombie Hornback he can see within 60
  feet to move up to its speed and make one Gore attack. He doesn't need line of command — a
  snapped order over the noise of a fight works fine.
- **Policy Lapse.** The first time Roy drops to half his hit points or fewer, every Zombie Hornback
  within 30 feet of him gains a burst of frantic, directionless violence: each makes one extra Gore
  attack against the nearest creature (friend or foe) at the start of its next turn. He finds this
  funny.

**Actions**
- **Claim Denied.** *Ranged Spell Attack:* +6 to hit, range 90 ft., one target. *Hit:* 14 (4d6)
  necrotic damage.
- **Audit (Recharge 5-6).** 30-foot cone. Each creature in the area makes a DC 14 Constitution
  saving throw. *Failure:* 21 (6d6) necrotic damage, and the target's hit point maximum is reduced
  by an amount equal to the damage taken, lasting until the target finishes a long rest. *Success:*
  half damage, no reduction.

**What ends this without a kill.** Roy isn't suicidal about his own relevance — if he's bloodied
and losing (most of the herd down, no path to winning), he breaks off, animates whatever's left of
the nearest corpse as a distraction, and runs. Agent loves a villain who gets away to come back
later more than it loves a clean kill. Leave him alive unless the table wants him gone for good.
