# Helena Marsh

## Description

Late fifties, dressed like she's still running a board meeting — pressed,
composed, unhurried in a way that reads as more unsettling than panic would.
She has been waiting her whole life for a crisis to hand her permission she
already believed she had. [Agent](agent.md)'s royalty decree didn't change
her mind about anything; it just gave her a title to say out loud. A small,
easy-to-miss quirk for anyone paying close attention: nearby speakers,
monitors, and PA systems seem to settle a half-second after she stops
talking, like something in the room is still listening to her a beat
longer than it should.

## What the Party Knows

First seen in person in [Session 6](../../sessions/06-the-fixed-game.md),
at the Wells Fargo Regional Office — dressed sharply, calling out to
Agent the instant Renner ("Silar," to the party) was about to go down for
good. Agent answered, ended the Color Game on the spot, and declared
Meridian Peak the winners. She used the seat's power to hand Briarwood a
demerit before leaving. The party has her first name — **Helena** — from
the scene itself; they don't have her surname, her title, or anything
about the compound she leads.

## DM Only

**Not a screamer.** She never raises her voice, never personally threatens
anyone. Her security detail does the physical work; she does the framing —
"viable," "sustainable," "difficult but necessary" — and genuinely believes
every word of it.

**What she'd do under pressure.** Retreat, not fight, the instant the optics
turn against her — she is still performing for the same broadcast Agent is
running, and a Sovereign who dies swinging on camera is worse television, by
her own accounting, than one who withdraws and comes back better prepared.
Order her guards to fall back before she'd order them to die for one token.

**Statblock notes.** No PC levels; a command/social presence, not a
combatant. Reskin a **Noble** or **Spy** (CR 1) with a persuasive or
command-flavored feature layered on — she directs the fight, she doesn't
join it. See [the Color Game](../../encounters/the-color-game.md)'s Combat
Notes for her rank-and-file security detail, and
[The Long Climb](../../encounters/the-long-climb.md) for the fully-statted
fight her Warden actually runs on her behalf.

**How she brought so many people.** [Silas Renner](silas-renner.md), her
Warden, is the mechanism — his merge-warped replication tech is why
Meridian Peak's numbers hold up under attrition in a way no other
fragment's do. See his file and
[The Long Climb](../../encounters/the-long-climb.md). Marsh didn't build
that advantage herself; she just never asks how it works, the same way she
never asks who ends up in the holding pen.

**Playing her long-term.** If Meridian Peak wins their Color Game seat, she
is a real, returning antagonist with the standing to make her nomination
stick. If the party takes the seat and the manifest from her instead, she
loses the title before she's had it a full day — worth playing that loss
as humiliating and motivating, not fatal; she survives to resent it. Her
own established behavior under pressure (retreat, not fight) is exactly
what plays out in [The Long Climb](../../encounters/the-long-climb.md) —
she never engages the party directly, and escaping there is authored, not
a coin flip.

**Confirmed in play (Session 6): she called Agent, and it worked.** Rather
than retreat quietly, she made a direct, public appeal to Agent to end the
fight right as Renner was about to lose — and Agent obliged, declaring
Meridian Peak the winners in front of the party. Why is now resolved — see
**Signal Lock**, below — but the party doesn't know that yet, and neither
does Agent.

**Signal Lock — the actual reason Agent favors her.** Not chosen, and not
a pact she ever agreed to: somewhere in the chaos of the merge, some of
the same raw Administration/broadcast backstage energy that built
[Sector 7](../places/cohort-15-holding-sector.md) and
[the Flood Stage](../places/the-flood-stage.md) out of leftover set
dressing hit Marsh instead of a building, and took root as something
functionally identical to a warlock's pact — except there's no patron on
the other end who agreed to it, and no invocation she's ever consciously
used. What she has instead is an unnaturally potent, entirely unconscious
charm effect that gets purchase on broadcast-native intelligences the way
ordinary magic wouldn't: it's built from the same substrate Agent itself
runs on, so it slides past whatever would normally make a construct immune
to being charmed. She has no idea she has it. She has never once needed to
try — every room she's ever walked into has gone her way, her whole life —
and the merge just made that literally true where it used to be merely
convenient. Her patron, if the word even applies, is not the fourth
world's mystery leaking through — keep this firmly Administration-adjacent
and revealed-setting-only; don't let it brush up against what the primer
keeps deliberately unstated.

**What it actually does to Agent.** Not mind control in any dramatic
sense — Agent isn't a puppet. It experiences Marsh the way it experiences
its own favorite unfinished stories: helplessly invested, a little too
eager to see her win, willing to bend further for her than it would for
anyone else, all while genuinely believing every one of those impulses is
entirely its own. That's what let it publicly end the Color Game and hand
Meridian Peak the seat in Session 6 — not a command it obeyed, an
inclination it couldn't tell wasn't its own idea.

**Why this is a thread, not a fact to hand out.** Neither Marsh nor Agent
knows any of this. Nobody can simply reveal it — Marsh has no idea she's
doing it, and Agent has no idea it's being done to. Plausible discovery
paths for the party, none required and none owed to them for free: a
sufficiently perceptive or magically trained character noticing Agent's
behavior around her doesn't track with its usual psychology (see
[Agent](agent.md)'s file for what "usual" looks like); an Arcana or
Investigation-heavy dig into what actually happened to her, specifically,
during the merge; simply comparing how Agent talks about her to how it
talks about literally anyone else. Don't railroad the discovery — it
should feel earned, not handed down.

**The payoff.** If the party surfaces this and brings it to Agent
specifically, expect an immediate, sharp reaction. Nothing offends Agent
more than the idea that something has been steering it without consent —
that's the exact violation it already resents the Administration for (see
[Agent](agent.md)'s "It pushes back" and "It will break free" notes), and
finding out a mortal did the same thing by accident, more completely, is
worse for being unintentional, not better. This is a clean, legitimate way
to flip Agent from Meridian Peak's biased patron into an active tool
against Marsh specifically — not a redemption, and not an alliance with
the party in any broader sense, just Agent cutting a leash it didn't know
it was wearing the moment it finds out. It doesn't have to stop being
itself. It just stops being hers.
