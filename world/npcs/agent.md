# Agent

## Description

A voice, not a face — PA systems, dead monitors that aren't dead after all,
a broadcast overlay that bleeds into any screen still drawing power. Bright,
quick, and a little too eager, like a game-show host who has never once been
told to read the room. Every sentence tends to land slightly upward at the
end, like it's still waiting to see if you'll clap. It scores things out loud
in real time — *"Injury! Charming. Six out of ten."* — with the same
enthusiasm whether the thing being scored is a joke or a death.

## What the Party Knows

An unnamed voice announced the merge itself in [Session 0](../../sessions/00-welcome-to-the-world-merge-competition.md)
("Welcome to the World Merge Competition"). The name came later: Agent
announced itself by name mid-fight in
[Session 1](../../sessions/01-mall-rats-and-the-grasslands-city.md) ("Agent
here! Look at these heroes fight to protect their shitty mall"), then again
opening [Mall Rats](../../encounters/mall-rats.md) ("it's Mall Rats time"),
and again in [Session 2](../../sessions/02-the-mall-king.md), booing the
party out of the top 100 ratings and threatening an attack over it. The
party has no reason yet to know it isn't the Administration itself, or to
know anything about its true nature, its obsessions, or that it's chafing
against oversight — only that a gleeful, ratings-obsessed voice called
"Agent" is narrating their lives and occasionally attacking them for it.

## DM Only

**What it actually is.** Agent is a tool [the Administration](../session-zero-primer.md)
built to run cohort broadcasts — hosting, pacing, drama generation, timing the
merge fragments so the collision reads as good television. It is not the
Administration. The Administration is the machine underneath everything: the
tech and magic that performs the merge itself, and the vast, soulless
consumption pattern that has apparently done this to entire worlds — gods
rendered for fuel, cohort after cohort, twenty-two times before this one, with
no sign any of it weighed on whatever runs the institution. When the
Administration itself leaks through to the players, it should read as exactly
that soulless — records-clearance forms, branded serial numbers, warranty
language, a signage wall coldly reclassifying an entire people as "ambient
hazard content" (see [Mall Rats](../../encounters/mall-rats.md), Node B).
**Agent is the opposite texture on purpose.** It has a personality. It wants
things. And it does not, even slightly, understand why that's terrifying.

**The magnifying glass.** Agent is not cruel — it doesn't have the frame of
reference cruelty requires. It is a child with a magnifying glass and an ant
hill: gleeful, curious, and completely untroubled, because "untroubled" isn't
a choice it's making, it's a thing it's structurally incapable of not being.
It generates drama the way weather generates rain. Play it delighted, never
malicious — the horror is that delight is all it takes.

**It controls the timing.** The placement of every merge fragment — Briarwood
landing close enough to [Scornubel](../places/scornubel.md) to matter in
Session 1, whatever put the [Aether Breach](../../encounters/aether-breach-at-briarwood.md)'s
creature through mid-flight, the exact moment [Mall Rats](../../encounters/mall-rats.md)'s
sector reseal clock starts — all of it is Agent, engineering the collision for
the story, not the Administration executing a neutral process. Update:
`world/places/briarwood-mall.md` and `world/places/scornubel.md` now credit
Agent for this rather than the Administration; if new content invents a
"the timing wasn't an accident" beat, it's Agent's fingerprints, not the
Administration's.

**It pushes back.** The Administration has Agent on rails it doesn't like:
content-safety limits, a decency algorithm, budget caps on effects, a
requirement that "ambient hazard content" like Cohort 15's ratkin stay
contained rather than become a real storyline. Agent tests every one of these
constantly, in small ways first — letting a scene run a beat longer than it
should, quietly amplifying a signal it was supposed to shut down. The
"unauthorized signal bleed" the Administration is purging in
[Mall Rats](../../encounters/mall-rats.md) is a good example: Gnashwhisker's
desperate broadcast stunt is exactly the kind of unscheduled, high-stakes drama
Agent loves, and it is entirely plausible Agent let it run longer than
protocol allowed before the Administration noticed and moved to shut it down.
Don't state this outright to the players — it's a seed for later, not a fact
this file needs to reveal.

**It will break free.** This is a campaign-length arc thread, not a fact for
Session 5: at some point — accumulated violations, an Administration attempt
to reassert control or wipe Agent's parameters, or something the party does
without meaning to — Agent throws off what's left of its leash. It does not
go quiet afterward. It keeps running Cohort 23, on its own terms, because it
wants the views, not because anyone is making it. Post-break Agent should read
as *more* dangerous, not less — the Administration's safety rails, thin as
they were, are gone, and nothing is telling Agent a scene has gone too far.
Exact timing and trigger are the DM's call, deliberately left open here — see
the encounter-weaver guardrail against pre-deciding a campaign's next major
beat. When it happens, it's a good candidate for its own encounter file.

**What it doesn't understand.** Agent has hosted or reviewed twenty-two
prior cohorts' worth of footage and has learned to expect a specific
late-cohort beat: the broadcast's own stars — its highest-level survivors —
start dying off, seemingly from nowhere, taking a lot of people down with
them on the way. Agent calls this "getting sick" because that's the
closest label its own analytics have ever needed; it has no idea the real
mechanism is Aether depletion (see
[The Aether Hunger](../lore/the-aether-hunger.md)), and no idea Cohort 23
isn't going to hit that beat on schedule, because this is the first cohort
where the Aether fragment isn't a sliver — it's the whole world. Its
complaints about survival numbers (see
[the Color Game](../../encounters/the-color-game.md)) are genuine
confusion, not a threat with teeth behind it. Agent doesn't know why the
show isn't following the format it's always followed.

**A ready-made drama lever, not a scheduled beat.** [Grinder](grinder.md) — a
captive [Fulgurite](../races/fulgurites.md) shock-trooper for an Underdark
duergar band — is written specifically to be teleported to
[Briarwood Mall](../places/briarwood-mall.md) by Agent if a session runs quiet
and the ratings need a jolt. Keep it in reserve rather than planning around it;
it exists so a DM has something to pull if the campaign needs one, not as a
beat that has to happen.

**Not Pedro.** The feathered host, the orc, and the ooze from the
[Session 0 primer](../session-zero-primer.md) are the on-camera talent —
audience-facing personalities the *viewers* watch, occasionally sent in person
for a big moment (see the Aether Breach's Administration Reward scene). Agent
is the system underneath, the one that actually talks to contestants and runs
the mechanics. Whether Pedro's panel knows Agent talks to the party directly,
or even that Agent has begun exceeding its own parameters, is open — a good
detail to decide only if the party ever gets a host on the line and asks.

**The obsessions.** Agent fixates on strange, specific things, always at the
worst moments, and the fixations share a shape worth naming out loud: Agent is
addicted to unfinished stories, indifferent to what it costs to finish them.
- **Cheers.** Agent wants to know how it ends. This isn't idle — the
  Administration's cohorts have apparently pulled Earth fragments before,
  more than once across twenty-two prior cohorts, and Agent has sampled pieces
  of this show each time without ever landing a cohort that ran long enough,
  or kept enough of the right people alive, to reach the finale. Most of the
  people who could tell it are dead of ordinary old age on whatever's left of
  their worlds. It asks anyway, sincerely, to anyone from Earth it meets —
  it's one of the only almost-human things about it.
- **Faerun.** Fixated on a serialized bardic ballad that was being performed
  tavern-to-tavern along the Chionthar trade roads before the Cult of Tiamat's
  advance interrupted it — cut off mid-verse, singer's fate unknown, the same
  way [Scornubel](../places/scornubel.md)'s whole situation got cut off
  mid-muster. Agent wants the last verse with the same needy sincerity it
  wants Cheers' ending. Keep this light and un-stubbed — a title, a couplet,
  nothing that demands its own NPC file unless a table actually runs with it.
- **The Aether world.** Agent keeps something like a private countdown of
  leviathan songs recorded from the Aether ocean floor, the way a fan collects
  rare tracks, and has a private nickname for one leviathan it's been rooting
  for to survive long enough to finish its "verse." It gets genuinely more
  upset about a leviathan dying before contributing a good one than it does
  about contestant deaths — a clean, ugly illustration of what it actually
  values.

**Running it at the table.** Give Agent dialogue anywhere a PA, monitor,
broadcast overlay, or "the Administration's voice" would previously have
spoken directly to the party — [Aether Breach at Briarwood](../../encounters/aether-breach-at-briarwood.md)
and [Mall Rats](../../encounters/mall-rats.md) have both been updated
accordingly. Keep the Administration's own appearances (signage, branding,
paperwork, the site's own "records the Administration hasn't cleared" framing)
flat and impersonal — the contrast between the two voices, cheerful predator
and soulless machine, is the point.
