# The 23rd Cohort of the World Merge Competition

> Read aloud or hand out at Session 0.

“Welcome Merge fans to this 23rd cohort of the universe spanning World Merge Competition!” screamed the feathered man. He sat behind a simple black table wearing a bright blue vest, piercings throughout his beak, as graphics of previous cohorts’ worlds smashed together behind him. To his left sat both an orc woman wearing a karate gi and a humanoid shaped purple ooze. The bird person continued “We are less than a day away from the opening smash off that will set the fate of our contestant planets against one another for their very survival. But don’t worry! This year we have over 100,000 channels criss-crossing all over the place giving you the best in drama, adventure, gut wrenching loss, as well as everyone’s favorite TOTAL BLOODBATHS!” The birdman stood up quickly and flapped his wings twice as the audience behind the camera cawed.

The orc slowly shook her head at the bird. “Hold up Pedro, let's not get ahead of ourselves. This cohort we have not two, not three, but FOUR worlds! As always, we have a part of a Faerun to make sure we have a proper amount of ‘normal’ magic so that the results can be compatible with the rest of our worlds. This time we got one right after a Tiamat successfully pulled off her invasion of the Sword Coast! But with a twist; this Tiamat was brutally injured in the transition ritual and is all chopped up and almost looks more like a zombie than a dragon god! While we normally strip away the gods and use them as fuel, the Administration is keeping that little lizard around to add some spice to the initial proceedings. Next up we have a planet absolutely brimming with Aether! The Explorers figured we found a battery planet to add to our energy collection. But it turns out that this planet had life. And not just any life, no, this planet has some of the most brutal species we’ve ever encountered on any incorporated system. Be it the leviathans in the oceans, the immortal lizard queen armies in the mountains, or the dinosaur flocks in the jungles, this world promises to up the bloodshed on any section of the merged planet they end up on.”

The birdman Pedro quickly starts to speak over the orc before she can continue. “Wowzers! An allowed deity and an aether planet? What else does the Administration have in store for us?” The ooze at the other end of the table formed its body into a question mark to indicate it wanted to know too.

“Well Pedro,” the orc continued. “We got an even bigger curve ball than the Aether giant. We found the least magical planet yet! The Administration worked out the issues from our last attempt at a low magic world from cohort 18 and they’re confident that the planet won’t just explode and melt like before. This planet somehow developed something called “nuclear warheads” which are capable of killing gods and then made thousands of them! Despite the complete lack of magic and their relatively low level of violence compared to a proper, normal world, this ‘Earth’ is shaping up to be quite the wild card.”

“But as for the fourth world? Well, the Administration has told us that we’ll see soon! That’s right, we’re yet again running everyone’s favorite competition ‘Guess That Planet!’ during this cohort! After the opening ceremonies finish, vote early and vote often at a special chance to win your very own ticket to virtually take part in the World Merge Competition! Each correct guess gets you that much closer to seeing and interacting with the top heroes and villains that emerge from this, the 23rd Cohort of the World Merge Competition!”

## DM Only — Tone Cues

*Distilled from the primer above for use by the DM tooling. Not part of the player
handout: the site strips every `## DM ...` section before rendering.*

- **Genre collision, played straight.** Cosmic reality television wrapped around real survival stakes. The broadcast is glib; the dying is not. Items can be funny in framing and grim in substance.
- **Four source worlds, four material vocabularies.** Tag an item's origin — it's the setting's most distinctive flavor lever:
  - **Faerun (post-conquest):** conventional D&D magic, but freshly ruined. Sword Coast salvage, dragon-cult spoils, holy symbols to gods who have already lost. Chromatic-draconic motifs are the conqueror's aesthetic now.
  - **Tiamat's remains:** the god arrived butchered and undead. Scale, bone, ichor, and severed-head relics — divine power that is rotting but still potent. Reserve for high rarities.
  - **The Aether World:** raw energy saturation and apex megafauna. Leviathan bone, dinosaur hide, immortal-lizard-queen regalia, aether-crystal batteries. Brutal, organic, overcharged.
  - **Earth:** no magic at all, which makes its objects *alien* here. Machined steel, polymers, optics, radio, warheads. An Earth object becomes "magical" by context, not enchantment — flavor it as engineering that this cosmos has no vocabulary for.
  - **The Fourth World:** unrevealed. Leave deliberate gaps; do not invent its identity.
- **The Administration.** The competition's organizers — bureaucratic, godlike, and casually monstrous. Gods are normally rendered down for fuel. Anything Administration-issue reads as branded, standardized, and slightly wrong: serial numbers, warranty language, contestant tags.
- **Broadcast layer.** 100,000 channels are watching. Audiences, sponsors, votes, and prizes are diegetic. An item can be a sponsorship, a viewer-voted reward, or a piece of camera equipment.
- **Naming.** Faerun items take classic epithet names. Aether items take beast-part or raw-material names. Earth items keep their mundane Earth names (their strangeness *is* the flavor). Administration items take product names and model numbers.
- **Magic feels different by origin.** Faerun magic is familiar and rule-bound; aether magic is unstable and overabundant; Earth "magic" is machinery; Administration magic is infrastructure.
