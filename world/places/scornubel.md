# Scornubel

## Description

> **BY ORDER OF THE COSTER COUNCIL — THE CARAVAN CITY MUSTERS**
> Every blade, every bow, every spell. Every hand that can hold a pike.
> Sellswords: the Council pays **gold**, coin on the barrel, no coster's scrip, no promises.
> Free companies, caravan guards, temple orders, hedge-wizards, and any fool brave enough — present yourselves at the **Council Yard** at dawn bell.
> **THE DRAGONS ARE COMING UP THE RIVER. THEY WILL NOT STOP AT THE WATER.**

The broadsheet is nailed to every post in the city, three deep in places, and nobody has taken one down in a tenday.

It is the twenty-second of **Eleint**, an hour past dawn, and the river fog has not lifted because the river fog does not lift anymore — it comes up the Chionthar thick and faintly sulfurous, and it tastes of coins left in the rain. Scornubel is a city of tents and wagon-yards that grew a stone heart, and right now the tents have swallowed the stone. Refugee canvas runs from the docks to the Reaching Stream ford. The whole city smells like woodsmoke, wet wool, ox, and the tar they are painting on the new palisade. Under that: the forge-noise. Six hundred hammers. It starts before the dawn bell and it does not stop.

The **autumn caravans did not come**. That is the fact underneath every conversation in every wagon-yard. Nothing has come up the road from the coast since Highharvestide — no wool, no ore, no salt, no word — and what did come instead were people, on foot, in the thousands, with the story that **[Baldur's Gate](baldurs-gate.md) has fallen** and that the thing which took it wore Tiamat's colors and left the walls standing on purpose. The Sword Coast is gone. Not besieged. Gone. And the **[Cult of Tiamat](../factions/cult-of-tiamat.md)** and the god's surviving children are no longer holding the coast — they are moving *inland*, up the Chionthar, and Scornubel sits at the exact place where the river stops being a road to somewhere else and starts being a road to here.

There are no walls worth the name. Scornubel never needed them; it was a market with a city attached, and the Council is presently trying to build in a season what other cities took centuries to raise. They will not finish. Everyone knows they will not finish. So the Council has spent its treasury on the only wall it can buy quickly, which is **people who fight for money** — and the criers have gone out as far east as the Dales and as far north as the caravan roads can carry a rider.

Which is to say: if you can hold a weapon, Scornubel has been calling your name for a month, and it is paying.

## Notable Locations

- **The Council Yard** — the old caravan-assembly ground, now the muster field. Tables under awnings, a Council clerk with a ledger, a paymaster's strongbox under guard, and a line that starts before dawn. Contracts are read aloud because half the line cannot read. This is where the party is hired.
- **The Council Hall** — the stone records building behind the Yard, where the Coster Council keeps its charters, contracts, and the refugee rolls. The driest, most defensible structure in the city, and lately the site of something nobody official wants to put a name to — see [Miriel Ashgrove](../npcs/miriel-ashgrove.md)'s log.
- **The Broadsheet Post** — the notice pillar at the yard's mouth, papered in muster bills, bounties on cultists, missing-kin notices from the coast, and free-company recruitment sheets undercutting each other by the hour. The single best place in the city to hang a hook.
- **The Wagon-Yards** — Scornubel's real substance: acres of ox-pens, wheelwrights, cooperages, and coster warehouses along the Chionthar's north bank, most of them now half-emptied of goods and full of sleeping refugees instead.
- **The Tent City** — the sprawl west toward the Reaching Stream ford. Coast refugees, no sanitation, no law but what the costers' hired men enforce, and a fever going through it that nobody has named yet. Rumor runs faster here than anywhere in the Heartlands.
- **The Dawnpost** — the river watch: a timber tower and a chain-boom at the bend downstream, manned around the clock by volunteers who watch the fog for wings. Two false alarms so far. The bell can be heard in the whole city.
- **The Palisade Works** — the unfinished earthwork and stake wall on the river side. Trenches, tar-pots, and press-ganged labor. It stops men. It does not stop a dragon and every laborer on it knows that.
- **The Shrine Row** — a stretch of the caravan road grown thick with travelers' shrines, now doing the best business in the city: Tempus, Tymora, Helm, Lathander, and a dozen roadside gods with no temple anywhere. Militant orders are recruiting off the same street the sellswords are. Cheap devotional stock is sold here by the handful — see [Tymora's Last Copper](../../items/tymoras-last-copper.md) for a plausible find.
- **The Deep Cellars** — the old cold-storage vaults under the coster warehouses, dug for grain and now quietly being surveyed as the place the city hides when the sky opens.

## What the Party Knows

Nothing. The party are Earth civilians who were in [Briarwood Mall](briarwood-mall.md) on a Thursday evening; they have never heard the word *Scornubel* and have no reason to. Everything above is what the city was in the hour before the merge, entirely offscreen. This section fills in only as they learn it — and the first thing they learn will not be the city's name, it will be the smell of it.

## DM Only

- **Timeline: this is pre-merge.** Everything above is Faerun before [Agent](../npcs/agent.md) touches it. Nobody in this city has any concept of the competition, the other three worlds, or the broadcast. Written as a snapshot of the last ordinary hour on the Faerun side, deliberately parallel to [Briarwood Mall](briarwood-mall.md) — but the party is never in it. No PC musters here.
- **Function: this is the neighbor.** Scornubel and a stretch of the surrounding Chionthar valley come through the merge and land *close to Briarwood*. That is its entire purpose at the table. The party does not visit Faerun; Faerun arrives, at walking distance, already mid-crisis and already armed. The pre-merge writeup above is not a location the players explore so much as the answer to "what the hell is that, and why does it have ten thousand soldiers in it."
- **The population is the point.** Briarwood holds roughly four hundred Earth civilians. Scornubel mid-muster holds a mustered army of sellswords, a merchant council with a treasury, thousands of coast refugees in a tent city, and a fever nobody has named. Whatever the party thinks their situation is, the neighbors outnumber them by an order of magnitude, are professionally violent, and were told a month ago that dragons were coming. First contact is the campaign's first real problem — decide before you run it whether the muster reads the mall as a threat, an omen, or salvage.
- **What actually comes over.** Decide the merge line and hold to it, because the party will map it. The Council Yard, the wagon-yards, the tent city, and the palisade works are the pieces that matter; the deep cellars matter enormously if anyone needs to hide. Whether the **river** comes is the biggest single call — the Chionthar means water, boats, and a route, and Bellcross has none of those. Whether the **Dawnpost** and its bell come over decides whether Scornubel still has an early-warning system pointed at a downstream that no longer exists.
- **The muster is the hook engine — pointed the wrong way now.** The Council Yard and the Broadsheet Post still generate adventure on demand, but the contracts on that board were written against a threat that is no longer upriver. A city that raised an army for a war that got cancelled mid-mobilization is a far more volatile thing than a city under siege. Ten thousand armed people, paid in gold that may no longer mean anything, with no enemy in front of them and a shopping mall on the horizon.
- **The war did not come with them — probably.** Scornubel was doomed on Faerun. Post-merge, the [Cult of Tiamat](../factions/cult-of-tiamat.md) and the coast it conquered are somewhere else on the merged world, or nowhere. This is the campaign's most useful lever: the city was saved from the dragons by the worst thing that has ever happened to it, and nobody in it believes that yet. Every soldier there is still braced for an attack from a downstream that no longer exists.
- **Tiamat is intact as of this snapshot.** As far as anyone in Scornubel knew in the hour above, a whole and ascendant dragon goddess owned the Sword Coast. What the merge does to her is not their knowledge and should not leak backward into this file — but it is *excellent* news to deliver to these people later, and a strong reason for a Scornubel expedition once the party is ready to leave the mall.
- **Cult infiltration came over too.** Assume the [Cult of Tiamat](../factions/cult-of-tiamat.md) had cells in the tent city; a refugee flood is the easiest cover in the Realms. Those cells merged with everyone else and are now the only people in the valley whose god might be reachable. A cult cell that finds out what happened to Tiamat before the Council does is a fine mid-campaign engine.
- **The [Coster Council](../factions/coster-council.md) survives and is a problem.** They are merchants who were buying a wall out of other people's bodies and intended to leave; the merge stranded them with a full treasury, a hired army, and nowhere to evacuate to. They will be the first organized power to grasp that Briarwood's contents are *goods*. The mall is, from their side of the line, an unguarded warehouse of impossible merchandise.
- **[Halric Ammakyl](../npcs/halric-ammakyl.md)** is your first-contact figure, not a patron. He was running the muster tables when the sky changed, he is the man at the front of the Faerun side who can be reasoned with, and he is the reason the first meeting is a conversation instead of a massacre. Whether he stays that reasonable depends on how the party handles hour one.
- **Broadcast layer.** A magicless mall of four hundred civilians dropped beside a mustered fantasy army is *precisely* the matchup [Agent](../npcs/agent.md) builds cohorts around, and the placement is not an accident — Agent timed this collision on purpose, the same way it times every fragment landing. Play it as an accident anyway. Nobody on either side of the line should suspect they were positioned until much later.
- **Distance is a dial you set once.** How far is the mall from the Council Yard — a hundred yards of parking lot, or a day's walk with unknown ground between? Close means contact in Session 1 and no time to prepare; far means the party gets a few sessions of scavenging Briarwood before the horizon acquires torches. Both are good. Pick before Session 1 and don't waffle, because the answer determines the entire shape of the first arc. Whatever the gap turns out to be, [the Drowned Chantry](drowned-chantry.md) — a flooded shrine to Eldath fused with a Bellcross park lake, home to the [Swanfolk](../races/swanfolk.md) — is one confirmed feature inside it, not the whole answer to what's out there.
- **The architectural bleed-through starts here, pre-merge.** Days before the merge itself, the Council Hall gains doors it never had and a sealed, windowless building appears overnight on Coopers' Row — see [Miriel Ashgrove](../npcs/miriel-ashgrove.md)'s log, [Deputy Registrar's Log — Scornubel](../lore/deputy-registrars-log-scornubel.md). This is the same phenomenon as [Briarwood's extra janitor's closet](briarwood-mall.md), running on both sides of the eventual merge line before the main event — Administration backstage leaking through on Agent's schedule, not the merge's. Use it as a clue players can connect only after they've found the mall's own extra closet.
- **Open question:** what does Scornubel think Briarwood *is*? A city that spent a month expecting the dragons, then had reality torn open, is now looking at a windowless slab full of light, humming, and unarmed people. Read as a fortress, it gets stormed. Read as a divine sign, it gets pilgrims. Read as a warehouse, it gets looted. That single interpretation is the campaign's opening move and it belongs to the DM.
