# Deputy Registrar's Log — Scornubel

> A found document. Hand it to the players as a recovered page — looted off a body,
> left behind at an abandoned registry table, or produced by [Miriel Ashgrove](../npcs/miriel-ashgrove.md)
> herself if she survives to be met in person.

**6th of Marpenoth. Written by rushlight — the good candles went to the fever tents.**

I told Warden Ammakyl this morning that if one more free company tries to charge a
"gate toll" to refugees entering the wagon-yards, I am going to start reading their
contracts back to them myself, aloud, at the Broadsheet Post, in front of whoever's
still standing in line. He laughed. He thinks I'm joking. I am not joking. I have
simply run out of the kind of tired where you still have the energy to sound angry
about it.

Here is the count, for whoever reads this after me, because someone should have
written it down honestly even if the Council won't hear it read aloud:

Since the coast fell, Scornubel has taken in — by my own tally, which is more
honest than the one on the Council's public boards — past six thousand souls, and
the tent city has stopped being something anyone can call temporary. I register
perhaps two hundred new names a day, more on bad days, and every one of them has a
story that starts with "we ran" and I have stopped asking them to finish it,
because they always do anyway, whether I ask or not.

We hired an army to keep the dragons out. What we got instead — since the dragons
have, thank every roadside god on the Shrine Row, not yet arrived — is several
thousand armed men and women standing around a city stuffed with frightened,
propertyless people, drawing Council coin whether they behave or not. I do not
think anyone on the Council sat down and decided that was the arrangement they
wanted. I think they hired swords to fight dragons, and dragons are not what
showed up to be fought, and nobody has updated the contracts to say what a
sellsword owes a refugee instead.

Three nights this tenday I have had refugees come to my table with the same
complaint, three different companies, same shape every time: a "protection fee"
collected at the ration line, paid in coin if you have it, in goods or worse if you
don't. I logged all three. I read Halric the conduct clause we put in every muster
contract — no extortion of civilians under Council protection, penalty forfeiture
of the tenday's pay — and asked when the Council meant to enforce its own paper. He
told me the Council can't afford to make an example of a company it still needs
behind the palisade next month. I told him the Council also can't afford six
thousand people deciding the mercenaries are worse than the dragons were. He didn't
have an answer for that one. I don't think there is one the Council likes.

So I keep my own ledger now, separate from the public rolls. Names, companies,
dates, what was taken. I don't know yet what I'm building it for — a tribunal that
will never convene, maybe, or just so that somebody, somewhere, has the true count
written down instead of the one that makes the Council look competent. Half-elf
clerks don't get seats on tribunals. But we can hold a pen, and nobody's told me to
stop.

I have not slept indoors in eleven days. I have not slept, more accurately, in
about four, if you don't count the kind where you close your eyes at the registry
table and open them again because someone is shouting about ration chits.

I am so tired. I want someone — anyone with more authority than a table and a
ledger — to walk in, look at what this city has become, and simply fix it. I know
that isn't coming. I am the closest thing this line of frightened people has to
someone on their side tonight, and some nights lately that feels like a very small
thing to be.

---

*Later — past the third bell, in a different hand's ink; I borrowed the nearest
quill.*

A runner came from the Council Hall while I was writing the above, and I almost
didn't let him finish before sending him back — I assumed it was a fourth
mercenary complaint and I did not think I had the strength for one tonight. It
wasn't.

He says the Hall has more doors in it than it did yesterday. Not new construction
— nobody has built anything, no mason's been near the place, and the two clerks
who sleep in the records wing swear to it — just doors, where there used to be
plain wall, unremarkable and identical to every other door in the building except
that nobody can say when they were hung. One of the wing clerks tried a handle he
swears wasn't there this morning, and there is a room now that connects the
archive corridor directly to the old dry-goods cellar — a walk that used to take
you outside and back in through two other doors. He is understandably rattled. He
wants me to file it as structural damage so a mason can be sent to look at it, as
though a mason is the trade you send for a door that grew.

And — this is the part I keep rereading to be sure I copied the runner down
right — there is a building on Coopers' Row that was not there at Highharvestide.
Nobody remembers commissioning it. Nobody remembers it being built. The tenants of
the shops to either side swear on their patron gods that the lot was bare two days
ago. It has no windows. It has one door, and the door is locked, and nobody in
this city holds the key.

I have six thousand refugees, no sleep, and a Council that will not enforce its
own contracts, and now apparently my own registry hall is growing rooms it did not
have last week. I am writing this down because someone should, and because I no
longer trust myself to remember, by morning, whether I imagined it or whether it
happened. If it is still there at dawn bell, I suppose I will have to go and look
at the door myself, though gods know when I will find the hour.

I am going to try to sleep for one bell before the dawn line starts. I do not
expect to.

— *M. Ashgrove, Deputy Registrar*

## What the Party Knows

Exactly what's written above and nothing more — the party has no context yet for
what a door that grows out of a wall, or an empty lot that grows a locked,
windowless building overnight, might mean. That recognition only arrives in
hindsight, once the party has met [Briarwood Mall](../places/briarwood-mall.md)'s
own extra janitor's closet on their own side of the line.

## DM Only

**Timeline.** Written days before the merge event described in the [Session 0
primer](../session-zero-primer.md), continuing the crisis snapshot in
[Scornubel](../places/scornubel.md) forward past its 22 Eleint muster date. Nobody
in this document has any concept of the competition, the other worlds, or the
broadcast — same rule as the parent place file.

**What this actually is.** The extra doors in the Council Hall and the sealed,
windowless building on Coopers' Row are the same phenomenon as
[Briarwood's extra janitor's closet](../places/briarwood-mall.md) — Administration
backstage bleeding through *ahead of* the main merge event, not caused by it. See
[Agent](../npcs/agent.md)'s note in the primer's tone cues: it controls the timing
of fragment landings, and "the placement wasn't an accident" belongs entirely to
it. Run this beat identically on both sides of the eventual merge line: an extra,
slightly-wrong door or structure that predates the main event and reads as
inexplicable rather than magical to the people who find it.

**What's behind the locked door.** Undecided on purpose, same as the open question
left in [Sector 7 (Cohort 14 Holding)](../places/cohort-14-holding-sector.md).
Plausible options: a second entrance into that same Administration storage
sector, giving a Faerun-side route into the space [Corey Blevins](../npcs/corey-blevins.md)
is trapped in; a wholly separate backstage cell; or nothing more than locked
stone until the party forces it, at which point it becomes whatever this campaign
needs it to be. Decide only if the party goes looking on the Scornubel side.

**Using this as a clue.** This document is meant to land twice. The first read is
just an overwhelmed civil servant's log — sympathetic, exhausting, human. It should
only click as *the same thing as the mall's extra closet* on a second read, ideally
after the party has already opened that closet or heard about [Mall Rats](../../encounters/mall-rats.md).
Don't editorialize the connection for them; let a player make the catch.

**How the party can encounter it.** Looted off an abandoned registry table if
Scornubel's line collapses before the party gets there; recovered from a body if
[Miriel Ashgrove](../npcs/miriel-ashgrove.md) doesn't survive contact; or handed
over personally by Miriel if she does, as her opening proof that she noticed
something was wrong before anyone else in the city did.
