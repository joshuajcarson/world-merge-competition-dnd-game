# Private Security Log — Briarwood Mall

**Thurs Nov 5. Centurion Protective Services — contract coverage, Briarwood Mall,
"As Seen On TV" grand opening/signing.**

**1:45 PM — Clocked in.** Mall security signed me in like they weren't sure
Centurion was a real company. It is. It has a logo. I have a laminated badge
with my name spelled right, which is more than Gary at the info desk can say
about his own nametag, and he's worked there four years. These guys walk the
promenade like they're doing a lap for exercise, not because anybody told them
to. I get it, I really do, eight bucks an hour doesn't buy urgency. Neither does
what Centurion pays me, if I'm honest, but at least I look like I take it
seriously, which is nine-tenths of the job.

**2:15 PM — First loop.** Radio Shack's still got the TV in the window on
election coverage, sound off, day three, like anybody's mind is changing on
Ross Perot at this point. Woman in Waldenbooks moved the Perot biographies to
the front table herself, no one asked her to, that's just what happens to
people in this town this week. Noted: kid loitering by the Suncoast with a
backpack too full for a guy who isn't buying anything. Didn't do anything
about it. Not my post, not my problem, and if he walks out with a VHS tape
under his jacket that's between him and whichever blue-vest actually gets paid
to care.

**3:10 PM — Food court.** Guy at Radio Shack counter and the girl running
Orange Julius keep doing this thing where they trade something across the
counter that isn't a cup and isn't a receipt, quick, practiced, like they've
done it every day this month, which they probably have. Could report it.
Could also finish my coffee before it's cold. I clock threats to the public, I
don't clock two mall employees running whatever side hustle keeps this place
in business, and anyway it's none of my department — Centurion's contract is
the event, not loss prevention for the whole promenade. Somebody else's
paycheck, somebody else's problem.

**4:05 PM — Line's forming.** Started outside Chess King like the flyer said
it would, already wrapping toward the arcade by four. Mall put up the good
stanchions, the retractable ones, which tells you somebody upstairs actually
cares how this looks on the news, assuming Bellcross news covers a woman
selling a thigh machine, which, God help us, it will. I did a headcount for my
own curiosity, gave up around two hundred, kept going anyway because there's
nothing else to do standing here.

**5:20 PM — Back-of-house, because my feet hurt and nobody walks back there.**
This is the part I keep chewing on and I don't love that I keep chewing on it.
I've covered this mall for Centurion three, four times a year since '90. I
know the service corridor. Loading dock, dumpster bay, the guard room with the
little black-and-white monitors nobody watches after six, mechanical room, one
janitor's closet per wing, four wings, four doors, I could draw it blind. I
went to lean against the wall by what I'd call the west-wing closet and for a
second — one second, and I've decided it doesn't count — I could've sworn
there were two doors there instead of one. Same paint, same hardware, same
everything, just one more of it than the hallway has room to make sense of. I
blinked, it was one door, and I am not going to be the contract girl who calls
it in and gets the "everything okay?" voice from her own supervisor over the
radio. My eyes are tired. I've been on my feet since quarter to two. That's
the whole incident report, if anybody ever asks, which nobody will, because
I'm not writing it up.

**6:40 PM — Getting into position.** Told myself I wasn't going to care about
this. I don't care about this. I positioned myself with a clean sightline to
the riser anyway, purely professional, purely for coverage purposes, and if
that sightline happens to also be the best seat in the house for the actual
signing that is a coincidence Centurion doesn't need to hear about.

**7:05 PM — She's out.** Suzanne Somers, live, in a blazer that's doing more
work than the ThighMaster ever will, and the fountain court crowd made a noise
I have genuinely never heard four hundred Bellcross residents make for
anything, including the Cowboys. First fifteen people through the line got
their totes. Somebody's kid is already crying about not being in the first
fifteen. My job, technically, is to watch the crowd, not the stage, and I want
the record to reflect that I am mostly doing that. Mostly.

— *B. Hollis*

## DM Only

**What the party knows.** Exactly what's written above and nothing more — a
private security guard's shift log, funny and a little pathetic, with one
paragraph that reads like nerves rather than evidence. Nothing here confirms
anything is actually wrong; it reads that way only in hindsight.

**Timeline.** Runs the same afternoon and evening as
[Briarwood Mall](../places/briarwood-mall.md)'s Session 0 writeup, ending
minutes into the actual signing — before the World Merge Event described in
the [Session 0 primer](../session-zero-primer.md). Brenda's shift, and this
log, stop well short of it.

**What the 5:20 PM entry actually is.** The same phenomenon as the
[Deputy Registrar's Log](deputy-registrars-log-scornubel.md)'s doors in the
Council Hall, mirrored onto the Earth side of the eventual merge line —
Administration backstage bleeding through *ahead of* the main event, exactly
as [Agent](../npcs/agent.md)'s tone-cue note describes. This is the same extra
janitor's closet [Briarwood Mall](../places/briarwood-mall.md)'s own DM notes
flag and that [Mall Rats](../../encounters/mall-rats.md) later opens onto — Brenda
saw it a few hours before it was fully load-bearing and talked herself out of
it. She does not investigate, does not open anything, and does not tell
anyone. That's the point of her as a character: sharp enough to catch it,
lazy and status-conscious enough to bury it rather than be the guard who filed
a weird report over a door.

**Using this as a clue.** Same doctrine as the Scornubel log: let it land
twice. Cold, this reads as a bored, funny, slightly mean shift log. It should
only click as corroborating evidence once the party has found the extra
closet themselves and wants to know whether anyone noticed it before they
did — at which point Brenda's blink-and-it's-one-door moment becomes the
detail that confirms they aren't imagining the mall's layout getting worse
over time. Don't hand them that reading; let them do the arithmetic.

**Brenda's fate and the Radio Shack/Orange Julius detail.** Both open on
purpose. Whether Brenda survives the merge is the same kind of call as
[Suzanne Somers](../npcs/suzanne-somers.md)'s — decide it against whatever the
table needs. The 3:10 PM entry is Brenda incidentally clocking
[The Circuit](../factions/the-circuit.md)'s handoff without understanding it
and choosing not to report it, which is consistent with the faction's whole
strategy of looking like nothing — treat it as color, not a new lead, unless
the party is already circling Wade Kessler's crew and a DM wants a second
confirming witness.

**How the party can encounter it.** Recovered from a Centurion clipboard left
in the back-of-house guard room if Brenda doesn't make it; handed over by
Brenda herself, defensively and with a lot of unnecessary justification, if
she does and the party corners her about what she saw before anyone else did.
