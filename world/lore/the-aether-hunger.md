# The Aether Hunger

## Overview

Why leveling actually works the way it does in this cosmos, why every prior
cohort of the World Merge Competition eventually turned violent from the
inside out, and why Cohort 23 might — nobody involved knows this yet — be
the first one that doesn't.

## What the Party Knows

Nothing directly. [Agent](../npcs/agent.md)'s passing comments about
"getting sick" and disappointing survival numbers (see
[the Color Game](../../encounters/the-color-game.md)) are the only surface
trace of this so far, and Agent itself doesn't understand what it's
describing well enough to explain it if asked.

## DM Only

**The real mechanic behind leveling.** Character advancement — in this
cohort, in every cohort — draws on ambient Aether: the same raw-energy
saturation the Aether World's own life runs on. A contestant who fights,
survives, and grows more capable is metabolizing Aether the way that
world's own megafauna do, just at a much smaller scale. The Administration
has never explained this to anyone on either side of the broadcast, because
it doesn't need contestants or hosts to understand the machinery, only to
perform inside it.

**Why every previous cohort turned on itself.** In all twenty-two prior
cohorts, the Aether fragment brought into the merge was a piece — a
battery, a sliver, never the whole world — and it depletes. Once a cohort
runs long enough for its strongest survivors to level meaningfully, the
ambient Aether those survivors need starts running out faster than it
replenishes. The result, every time: the broadcast's own stars — its
highest-level, most capable contestants — start running out of the fuel
their own growth requires, and go looking for more of it wherever they can
get it. Not a plague, not a monster. Just increasingly desperate,
increasingly powerful people, starving for something invisible, killing
everything nearby that might be carrying some. Agent has watched some
version of this happen in every cohort it's hosted or reviewed footage of.
It has never known why. It just knows the ratings for that phase are
extraordinary — and it calls the phase, inaccurately, "getting sick."

**Why Cohort 23 is different, and nobody knows it yet.** This cohort didn't
get a fragment or a battery of the Aether World. It got the whole thing —
oceans, leviathans, immortal lizard queens, dinosaur flocks, all of it,
entire. There's no depletion happening because there's nothing here running
low. The die-off Agent expects, has built its own commentary around, and is
visibly irritated by isn't coming. Nobody involved knows why yet: not
Agent, which has no visibility into Aether reserves and is pattern-matching
off twenty-two prior cohorts' worth of instinct; not the Administration's
viewers, trained by past seasons to expect this beat and asking where it
is; not the 23rd Cohort's own contestants, who have no way to know
"leveling up" was ever supposed to have a cost, let alone what that cost
usually looks like.

**Playing this at the table.** A slow-fuse campaign secret, not a
Session-6 reveal. Agent's "getting sick" comments are the only surface
trace so far, and Agent itself doesn't understand what it's describing well
enough to explain it even if asked directly — a confused, cheated-feeling
host is the tell, not a monologue. Let the party notice the pattern in
Agent's own confusion long before anyone hands them the mechanism. The
payoff, whenever it lands: every level-up the party has ever had was, by
the Administration's own design, supposed to be costing someone else
theirs.

**Open thread.** Whether the whole Aether World eventually depletes anyway
— just on a timescale too slow to matter for this campaign — or whether it
genuinely never runs out, is deliberately undecided. A strong long-term
lever, not a fact this file commits to.
