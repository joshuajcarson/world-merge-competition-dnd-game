# Fulgurites

## Description

A separate Earth fragment from the same merge that stranded the party at
[Briarwood Mall](../places/briarwood-mall.md) — not the same people, not the same
place, just the same night, for whoever it happened to. This piece came down
somewhere in Faerun's Underdark instead of on the surface, and instead of a mall
full of shoppers it was however many people were in whatever building or vehicle
crossed the line: an unlucky handful, probably, not four hundred. There is
plausibly no surviving record, anywhere, of how many there were originally.

The name is what the Underdark's other residents call them, not what they call
themselves — a fulgurite, on Earth, is what you get when lightning strikes sand
and fuses it into glass in an instant, permanent and brittle where it used to be
loose. That's the metaphor the name is reaching for: something ordinary, hit once
by something enormous, and changed forever in the time it takes to happen. They
did not evolve into this over generations the way the Underdark's native peoples
did. They were normal people a few days or weeks ago, and then the ambient magic
radiation the Realms calls *faerzress* — background weirdness that reshapes a
species only very slowly, when it does anything at all — hit a population with
zero accumulated resistance to it, all at once, and did to them in days what it
usually does in centuries.

They are not organized. There is no Fulgurite nation, no shared language beyond
whatever English the survivors already had, no leadership beyond whoever's oldest
or loudest in a given knot of them. What exists are small, feral, frightened
bands, moving through tunnels that were never built for anyone their size, armed
with whatever came through the seam with them and whatever they've scavenged
since. Encountered singly, a Fulgurite reads as tragic. Encountered as a band
that's decided the party looks like a threat first, they read as exactly what
the Underdark's other predators already treat them as: dangerous, unpredictable,
and worth killing before they go off.

## Racial Trait

**Overcharge.** The first time a Fulgurite drops to half its hit points or fewer
in a fight, it discharges — not a choice it makes, a thing that happens to it.
When it triggers, pick or roll one:
1. **Force surge.** Each creature within 10 feet must succeed on a DC 13
   Strength saving throw or be pushed 10 feet and knocked prone.
2. **Psychic scream.** Each creature within 10 feet must succeed on a DC 13
   Wisdom saving throw or be frightened of the Fulgurite until the end of its
   next turn.
3. **Arc-light.** Each creature within 10 feet must succeed on a DC 13
   Constitution saving throw or be blinded until the end of its next turn.

This happens once per Fulgurite per encounter, whether or not the player or DM
running it wants it to. Play it as involuntary and a little frightening to the
Fulgurite too — a seizure with a blast radius, not a power move. A group of
Fulgurites overcharging in the same fight, in sequence, should read as chaos
escalating, not a coordinated combo; they don't plan this and can't chain it on
purpose.

**Reskinning note:** layer this onto whatever tier-appropriate humanoid base
statblock the scene calls for — the trait is what makes a Fulgurite a Fulgurite,
not its base combat stats. See individual entries under Notable Examples for
specific tiers once they exist.

**As loot:** see the [Fulgurite Splinter](../../items/fulgurite-splinter.md) —
a single-use item cut from a spent Fulgurite that lets whoever crushes it
trigger one Overcharge effect themselves. A grim, plausible drop from any
encounter here where a Fulgurite doesn't walk away.

## Notable Examples

- [Priya Anand](../npcs/priya-anand.md) — "the Conductor," a former air-traffic
  controller who kept most of her mind and now leads by force of will; can
  trigger other Fulgurites' Overcharge on command, without them needing to be
  bloodied first.
- [Grinder](../npcs/grinder.md) — a captive shock-trooper for the
  [Ashvein Company](../factions/ashvein-company.md), a duergar raiding band
  that has learned to pre-trigger his Overcharge before sending him in. Held
  in reserve as an [Agent](../npcs/agent.md)-triggered surprise arrival near
  [Briarwood Mall](../places/briarwood-mall.md) if the campaign needs a
  ratings-driven escalation.
- [The Chorus](../npcs/the-chorus.md) — several people fused into one body by
  the same strike that made everyone else an individual Fulgurite. A
  solo-boss-tier encounter built around Compound Overcharge: a fresh discharge
  every time it crosses a quarter of its max HP instead of just once.

## What the Party Knows

Nothing. The party has no reason yet to have heard of the Underdark, let alone
what's loose in it. This entry exists for the DM's use whenever that changes.

## DM Only

**Timeline.** Same merge event as the [Session 0 primer](../session-zero-primer.md)
and [Briarwood Mall](../places/briarwood-mall.md) — [Agent](../npcs/agent.md) placed
this fragment too, somewhere in the Underdark beneath whatever piece of Faerun
came through. Some amount of in-world time has passed since — enough for
faerzress exposure to finish doing what it did, which should read as longer than
"a few hours" and shorter than "generations." Pin down an exact span only if the
table needs one; "recently, but not immediately" is enough to run with.

**Why the Underdark and not the surface.** No hard answer needed — Agent times
fragment placement for drama (see the primer's tone cues), and "a magicless
population dropped somewhere this hostile" is exactly the kind of matchup it
finds compelling. Whether it did this on purpose or the merge's targeting is
just that imprecise is a fine open question to leave open.

**Connection to the party's own campaign.** Not established, and doesn't need to
be forced. [Scornubel](../places/scornubel.md)'s Deep Cellars — already being
surveyed as a place to shelter "when the sky opens" — are a plausible, low-cost
route to eventually connect this race to the party's actual geography if a
Underdark arc becomes useful later; treat that as one option among several, not
a commitment made by writing it down here.

**Tone.** Play them as a mirror of the party's own situation gone worse, not as
straightforward monsters — same merge, same lack of consent, much worse luck.
A table that takes a moment to notice that before the first Overcharge goes off
is getting the intended read. That said, don't undersell the danger: a
frightened band with an involuntary blast trait is still a real threat, and
Underdark survival has presumably made them fast to treat any stranger as
another predator.

**Open question:** challenge tier. Deliberately left unset in the frontmatter —
decide it against whatever party level actually needs a Fulgurite encounter,
the same way [Mall Rats](../../encounters/mall-rats.md) fixed the Ratkin's tier
only once a level 2 party was the confirmed audience.
