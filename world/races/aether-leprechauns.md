# Aether Leprechauns

## Description

Not leprechauns. The name is the table's own joke, not an in-fiction one —
what actually raids a fragment in "a Luck of Leprechauns" is a small,
wiry, unmistakably wrong humanoid population native to the Aether world,
green-skinned in the way mold is green, with too many teeth and hands built
for grabbing rather than anything more dexterous. They travel in loose,
opportunistic bands wherever a merge fragment has landed something shiny,
and they are drawn to two things without exception: gold, and anything
warm-blooded standing between them and it.

Unlike the Warrenwrights, they build nothing and leave nothing behind —
no warrens, no infrastructure, no evidence they were ever anywhere once
they've stripped it of coin and moved on. Unlike the Fulgurites, they
aren't tragic; nothing happened to them, this is just what an Aether
predator that specializes in scavenging looks like when it's smart enough
to use a bow.

## Racial Trait

**Gold Hunger.** An Aether Leprechaun always knows the direction and rough
distance of the largest exposed source of gold within 300 feet, the way a
shark tracks blood. If gold is visible and unclaimed, a Leprechaun will go
for it over any other objective — including a fight already in progress.
This is a genuine, exploitable distraction: a thrown handful of coins can
pull one, or several, off the party entirely.

**Ravenous Bite.** Every Aether Leprechaun, armed or not, will close to
bite rather than just shoot or slash if a target is bloodied or looks
easy — see Notable Examples for the base statblock this layers onto.

**The Doombow (subset only).** Not every Leprechaun in a raiding band
carries one — see Notable Examples — but the ones who do are the reason a
"Luck" is dangerous at range, not just up close. A Doombow is an
Aether-grown weapon, warped wood and something like sinew, that doesn't
just wound: a hit leaves the wound refusing to close all the way, a
lingering doom rather than ordinary damage (see Notable Examples' stat
block for the mechanic).

**Reskinning note:** as with any race entry here, layer these traits onto
whatever tier-appropriate humanoid base statblock the scene needs. Goblin
is the suggested baseline per the challenge band above — Nimble Escape is
a good fit for how skittish and opportunistic they are.

## Notable Examples

- [Gildrot](../npcs/gildrot.md) — the "Luck" raiding
  [the Preening Grounds](../places/the-preening-grounds.md); the one who
  actually carries a Doombow worth naming, and the closest thing the band
  has to a leader.
- [Prismrot](../npcs/prismrot.md) — a rare, prism-eyed variant whose luck
  manifests as raw, unpredictable elemental damage instead of a nose for
  gold. Not tied to any specific Luck yet — reuse it wherever the table
  wants a stranger kind of leader than Gildrot.
- The rank and file have no names and don't need any — a Luck is a mob,
  not a cast of characters, and most Leprechauns a party fights will never
  come up again.

## DM Only

**Why "Leprechauns."** Purely the table's own naming convention for "a
group of X," the same device as "a murder of crows" — there is no
in-fiction Earth-leprechaun mythology bleeding through here, and nothing
about these creatures should read as Irish folklore. If a player makes the
joke at the table, let them; don't let it become a genre expectation
(pots of gold, rainbows, wishes) the encounter then has to subvert.

**Tone.** Play them as vermin with a bow, not as a tribe or a culture —
closer to Mall Rats' Skulkers in register than to the Fulgurites' tragedy
or the Warrenwrights' competence. They're a hazard the fragment has, not a
people with a grievance.

**Open question:** whether Leprechaun bands recur elsewhere on other
Aether-touched fragments, or whether this specific Luck is a one-off for
[the Preening Grounds](../places/the-preening-grounds.md), is undecided —
reuse them freely if a table likes the fight.

see `.claude/skills/campaign-chronicle/references/monster-art-style.md`.
