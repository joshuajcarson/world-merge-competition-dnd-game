# Warrenwrights

## Description

Aether isn't gentle to anything that lives on it. The primer's own pitch is
leviathans in the oceans, immortal lizard queens in the mountains, dinosaur
flocks in the jungles — apex things that survive raw aether saturation by
being big, armored, or effectively unkillable. Warrenwrights survive it by
being none of those. Stocky, thick-clawed, chisel-toothed things the size of
a large dog, built low and heavy for digging, with a pelt that reads part
fur, part bark. They survive the same ambient energy that grows leviathans by
never holding onto it — every Warrenwright is built to shed charge into the
ground almost as fast as it absorbs it, and a colony's mounds, dams, and
tunnel networks are the visible waste-product of that constant bleed-off,
generation over generation, until the terrain itself is basically colony
infrastructure with grass on top.

That's the trait that survived the merge intact regardless of what world a
colony lands on. A Warrenwright doesn't experience "reshaping the
environment" as a project. It experiences unspent charge as something between
an itch and a countdown, and digging, damming, or gnawing is how the itch
stops. Drop one in a place with nothing to ground the charge into and it will
find something — which is exactly what makes them dangerous outside Aether:
a colony a few days into a new world has usually already rewired a
non-trivial chunk of it.

Colonies are matriarchal and highly cooperative — a Warrenwright alone is
uncomfortable and quieter than the stat block suggests; a colony at full
strength coordinates like a single distributed animal, sentries barking
alarm-clicks that the rest of the warren reacts to before a threat is fully
visible. Every colony has exactly one queen, called a Broodqueen locally and
whatever Cohort 23's broadcast has decided to nickname her wherever cameras
have found one. She doesn't fight. She doesn't need to — she's the reason the
rest of the colony fights as well as it does.

## Racial Trait

**Groundwork.** A Warrenwright that spends at least 1 minute uninterrupted in
an area begins instinctively reshaping it — burrows, gnawed barricades,
diverted water, stripped wiring, whatever the local material allows. Treat
this as a passive process the DM narrates into any encounter site a colony
has held for more than a few minutes: expect difficult terrain, improvised
cover, and at least one exploitable structural quirk (a chewed support, a
flooded low point, a collapsed-looking mound that isn't) before the party
ever sees a Warrenwright.

**Release.** The banked charge behind Groundwork has to go somewhere. The
first time a Warrenwright in a shaped area drops to half its hit points or
fewer, or the Broodqueen orders it (see Notable Examples), it collapses part
of its own construction on purpose — a controlled discharge, not a
tantrum. Effect and DC are drawn from the local environment; see
Environmental Adaptations below for three ready-made versions. In a setting
none of those cover, reskin it as a single burst that damages or displaces
creatures in a 10-foot area of the Warrenwright's own terrain, save DC 12,
scaled up for tougher specimens.

**Sentry Chorus.** While three or more Warrenwrights are within 30 feet of
each other, none of them can be surprised, and each has advantage on attack
rolls against a creature that doesn't have another ally within 5 feet of it —
the colony reads an isolated target as easy and swarms it. This is the
prairie-dog half of the trait: constant low-effort vigilance, not the big
one-time payoff Groundwork/Release is.

**Reskinning note:** as with any race entry here, layer these three traits
onto whatever tier-appropriate beast/humanoid base statblock the scene needs.
Gnoll is the suggested baseline per the challenge band above — Rampage
optional, Groundwork/Release/Sentry Chorus are what make it a Warrenwright.

## Environmental Adaptations

The same colony reflex reads very differently depending on what's on hand to
grind up. These three are ready to drop in as written; invent more the same
way — ask what the local environment has a lot of, then ask what a beaver
and a prairie dog would do with it.

### Aether Woods (home ground)

Full-strength Aether saturation, unlimited timber, and a colony that's had
generations to perfect the local warren. This is what a Warrenwright looks
like with nothing holding it back.

- **Living cover.** Difficult terrain and half-cover are the default
  anywhere a colony has been established more than a day — root-lattice
  floors, packed earthworks, gnawed deadfall.
- **Charge-fed regeneration.** While within 5 feet of colony construction (a
  mound, dam, or tunnel mouth), a Warrenwright regains 1d4 hit points at the
  start of its turn — the ambient aether the terrain is built to bleed keeps
  topping it off.
- **Release — Rootcollapse.** A support root-lattice gives way on purpose.
  Each creature in a 10-foot cube the Warrenwright designates must succeed on
  a DC 13 Dexterity saving throw or take 2d6 bludgeoning damage and be
  restrained until it or an ally uses an action to dig it free.

### Faerun River (Coster Council territory, or any river town)

Faerun's ambient magic is thin compared to home, but it's magic, and a river
gives a colony everything a beaver's instincts actually want: current to
redirect, banks to undercut, a channel to dam.

- **Amphibious.** Swim speed equal to walking speed; can hold its breath for
  10 minutes.
- **Borrowed current.** Once per short rest, a Warrenwright can shape a
  trickle of Faerun's ambient magic the way it would shape water — cast
  *shape water* (as the cantrip) with no components, flavored as diverting
  the river itself rather than casting a spell.
- **Release — Floodbreak.** The colony's dam gives way on cue. Each creature
  in a 15-foot line the Warrenwright designates, starting at the dam, must
  succeed on a DC 13 Strength saving throw or be knocked prone and pushed 15
  feet downstream; the line becomes difficult terrain (ankle-deep, then
  draining) for 1 minute afterward. Great as a mid-fight terrain reset — the
  encounter's geography is different after it triggers.

### Earth Casino (Bellcross side, or wherever a colony's gone feral indoors)

No ambient magic on Earth at all — the primer is explicit about that — so a
displaced colony grounds itself on the next best thing a casino floor has in
excess: electricity. Slot machine wiring, neon conduit, and a building's
worth of romex read to a Warrenwright exactly like root systems do at home,
and they gnaw and rewire accordingly. This is the one Agent's cameras love
most; a colony quietly rewiring a casino's guts is exactly the kind of
"nobody noticed until the lights did something wrong" beat the broadcast is
built for.

- **Live-wire claws.** A Warrenwright that's spent an hour or more in a
  wired building can deliver a bonus 1d4 lightning damage on its bite or
  claw attack, drawn straight from tapped building current.
- **Blackout reflex.** As a bonus action once per short rest, a Warrenwright
  can kill power to a 30-foot radius around itself for 1 round by biting
  through the nearest conduit — lights, machines, and anything
  electronic in the area go dark. Great for a clean retreat into vents and
  wall cavities the colony has already chewed through.
- **Release — Short Circuit.** The colony overloads a breaker on purpose.
  Each creature within 10 feet of the triggering panel, junction box, or
  machine bank must succeed on a DC 13 Dexterity saving throw or take 2d6
  lightning damage and be blinded until the end of its next turn by the arc
  and the shower of sparks. The immediate area loses power for the rest of
  the encounter.

## Notable Examples

- [The Landlady](../npcs/the-landlady.md) — a Broodqueen, whichever colony's
  matriarch the DM needs; can order a Release from any Warrenwright in her
  colony without that Warrenwright needing to be bloodied first, the same
  way [Priya Anand](../npcs/priya-anand.md) can trigger a Fulgurite's
  Overcharge.
- [The Sapper](../npcs/the-sapper.md) — a river-adapted worker found well
  outside home territory, on Faerun's side of the merge; the field example
  for the Floodbreak Release above.
- [The Pit Boss](../npcs/the-pit-boss.md) — a colony scout gone feral inside
  an Earth casino, rewiring the building one machine at a time; the field
  example for the Short Circuit Release above.

## What the Party Knows

Nothing yet — the party hasn't been placed anywhere near an Aether fragment.
This entry exists for whenever that changes, or whenever a displaced colony
turns up somewhere it has no business being.

## DM Only

**Why they travel well.** Groundwork/Release doesn't require Aether's raw
saturation to function, just *some* ambient energy to ground — which is why
a colony can end up feral and functional on Faerun or even magic-less Earth
(scavenging electrical current instead) without needing a Fulgurite-style
transformation event. That's a deliberate contrast with the Fulgurites: those
are ordinary people changed once, involuntarily, by exposure. Warrenwrights
were always built to process ambient energy this way — displacement doesn't
change what they are, just what they're chewing on.

**Broadcast angle.** Agent loves these — a colony quietly rewiring a
location over several episodes, unnoticed until Release triggers, is exactly
the "let a scene run a beat longer than it should" instinct described in
[Agent](../npcs/agent.md)'s DM Only section. The nicknames (Landlady, Sapper,
Pit Boss) are in-universe broadcast branding, not what the colony calls
itself — reuse the device from [Fulgurites](fulgurites.md) if inventing more
named individuals: an outside name, given by whoever's currently narrating
them.

**Tone.** Not tragic like the Fulgurites — Warrenwrights aren't victims of
the merge, they're opportunists who travel fine. Play a colony as
competent and a little unsettling: the party should be able to tell,
retroactively, that something had been quietly restructuring a location for
a while before the first Warrenwright ever shows itself.

**Open question:** which of the three sample sites (or a new one) actually
gets used is unset on purpose — pick whichever the party's next Aether-
adjacent location calls for, the same way challenge tier gets fixed only
once a real encounter needs it.
