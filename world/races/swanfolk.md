# Swanfolk

## Description

Two unrelated bodies of water used to occupy the same patch of ground for one
impossible instant, and neither of them survived it as what it was.

On the Faerun side, a modest stone chantry to **Eldath** — goddess of quiet
places, groves, and still water — sat off the Reaching Stream a comfortable
walk from [Scornubel](../places/scornubel.md), tended by a small order of
**swanmays**: novices and one elder, sworn protectors who could shift between
human and swan form at will. On the Earth side, an ordinary municipal park
outside Bellcross had an ordinary decorative lake, a gazebo, paddle boats,
and a resident flock of mute swans some local news segment had made into a
minor point of civic pride. Neither place had anything to do with the other.
Then the merge landed both waters in the same hole at once, and the two
flocks — one sacred, one simply municipal — came apart and reassembled as one
thing, at the exact moment several swanmays were mid-shift and several real
swans were mid-flight.

What settled out is neither swanmay nor swan: humanoid enough to walk the
chantry's flooded nave, feathered enough that "person" is the wrong word to
use around them, and carrying just enough of Eldath's residual, groveward
sanctity to be genuinely dangerous to provoke. They don't speak — whatever
language the swanmays had, the fusion didn't keep it — but they understand
threat, trespass, and grief with total clarity, and they have decided,
without anyone asking them, that the flooded ruin is theirs to keep and
everyone else is an intruder in it.

[Scornubel](../places/scornubel.md)'s refugees who've glimpsed them from the
ford call them **the Pen** — the old collective noun for a group of female
swans, repurposed as a name for the whole population, sacred and profane at
once. Nobody on the Briarwood side has a name for them yet.

## Racial Trait

**Water's Grace.** A Swanfolk has a swim speed equal to its walking speed and
treats shallow water and reeds as neither difficult terrain nor cover-denying
— it moves through its own territory the way nothing else in the encounter
can. While in water or within 5 feet of it, a Swanfolk can't be surprised.

**Reed-Song.** Once per short rest, a Swanfolk can loose a long, wavering cry
that isn't quite a swan's and isn't quite a hymn. Each creature within 30
feet that can hear it must succeed on a DC 13 Wisdom saving throw or become
charmed for 1 minute: not compelled to approach, just unwilling to be the one
who leaves the water's edge first. A charmed creature can repeat the save
whenever it or an ally takes damage. This is Eldath's own "you are safe here,
stay" made into a leash instead of a comfort — the fusion kept the feeling
and lost the kindness behind it.

**Wingstorm.** The first time a Swanfolk drops to half its hit points or
fewer, it rears up and thrashes in a territorial burst it doesn't consciously
choose to trigger. Each creature within 10 feet must succeed on a DC 13
Strength saving throw or be pushed 10 feet and knocked prone by the wingbeat.
Once per Swanfolk per encounter, same shape as
[Fulgurite](fulgurites.md) Overcharge and
[Warrenwright](warrenwrights.md) Release — reskin onto whatever
tier-appropriate base statblock the scene needs; this trait, Water's Grace,
and Reed-Song are what make a Swanfolk a Swanfolk, not its base combat stats.

## Notable Examples

- [Rosalind Voss](../npcs/rosalind-voss.md) — a Bellcross parks-department
  employee who was feeding the flock at the water's edge when the merge hit;
  kept enough of herself that she's the closest thing this population has to
  a voice that can be reasoned with.
- [Sister Maerwyn](../npcs/sister-maerwyn.md) — a swanmay novice caught
  mid-shift at the chantry's reflecting pool, permanently arrested between
  forms; the source of the flock's Reed-Song and the most dangerous single
  thing in [the Drowned Chantry](../places/drowned-chantry.md).

## What the Party Knows

Nothing yet. Neither Briarwood nor Scornubel has had reason to go looking in
the flooded ground between them.

## DM Only

**Timeline.** This happened in the same instant as the main merge, not
before or after it — the chantry and the park didn't collide with each other
so much as get forced to occupy the same coordinates, and the Swanfolk are
what was standing in the overlap. There's no "recently, but not immediate"
runway the way there is for the Fulgurites; this population has existed for
exactly as long as the merged world has.

**Tone.** Grief wearing a territorial instinct, not malice. Play them the way
you'd play an animal defending a nest that used to also be a chapel: fast to
threaten, fast to break off if the threat leaves, uninterested in pursuit
once intruders are back on dry ground. Reed-Song should read as sad more than
sinister at the table — it's trying to keep people safe by the only broken
logic it has left.

**Connection to the campaign's open geography.** Both
[Scornubel](../places/scornubel.md) and
[Briarwood Mall](../places/briarwood-mall.md) leave "what's actually in the
ground between the two" as a DM dial to set before Session 1. This race and
[the Drowned Chantry](../places/drowned-chantry.md) are one answer to that
question, not the whole answer — treat the chantry as one feature inside
whatever larger stretch of unclaimed ground the DM has decided sits between
the mall and the city, not a claim about the entire distance.

**Open question:** whether any Swanfolk can be brought back toward something
closer to their swanmay half — a *remove curse*-adjacent hook, a ritual at
the reflecting pool, or nothing at all — is deliberately unresolved. It's a
strong long-game reward for a party that deals with
[Sister Maerwyn](../npcs/sister-maerwyn.md) gently instead of just killing
her.
